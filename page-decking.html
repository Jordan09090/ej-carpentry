Decking Built for Perth's Climate

Perth's dry summers, strong UV and sandy soils are hard on a poorly built deck — boards cup, fixings work loose, and frames can shift if they weren't engineered properly in the first place. We design every deck around correct spans, ventilated subframes and hidden fixings, so it stays flat, drains well and feels solid underfoot for years, not just for the first summer.

Whether it's a full new build, an extension to an existing deck, or bringing a tired old deck back to life, we work from hardwoods like jarrah and merbau through to low-maintenance composite boards — matched to your budget, your home's style and how the space will actually be used.

Timber vs Composite Decking: Which Is Right for You?

Both perform well in Perth — the right choice usually comes down to how much upkeep you want to do and the look you're after. Here's how they compare:

Timber DeckingComposite Decking
Look & feelWarm, natural grain that ages and weathers over timeConsistent colour and finish that doesn't change much
MaintenancePeriodic oiling or staining to keep its colourOccasional clean — no oiling or staining needed
Upfront costGenerally lower material costHigher material cost, offset by lower upkeep
DurabilityVery durable when installed and maintained correctlyHighly resistant to splintering, warping and fading
Best forHomeowners who like a natural, evolving timber lookBusy households wanting a low-maintenance, set-and-forget deck

Decking FAQs

How much does a new deck cost in Perth?

Deck cost depends on size, timber or composite choice, height off the ground, and access. We quote each deck individually after a quick look at photos or an on-site visit, so you get an accurate, itemised price rather than a generic estimate.

Timber or composite decking: which is better for Perth?

Both work well in Perth's climate. Timber gives a warmer, natural look but needs periodic oiling; composite costs more upfront but needs almost no maintenance and won't splinter, warp or fade as fast in the sun.

Do I need council approval for a deck in WA?

It depends on the deck's height, size and location relative to boundaries — many low-level decks don't need approval, but higher decks or those close to boundaries often do. We can advise on this as part of your quote.

Can you repair an existing deck instead of rebuilding it?

Yes — loose boards, soft or rotten sections, sagging frames and worn fixings can usually be repaired without a full rebuild. We assess the subframe first and recommend a repair where it's structurally sound to do so.
